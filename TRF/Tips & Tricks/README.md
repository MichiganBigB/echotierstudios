# TRF — Tips & Tricks

Living production handbook for The Rhythm Foundation, EchoTierStudios. Established 2026-11-27.

## Musical foundations
Rhythm, intervals, scales, chords, harmony and temperament. Develop motifs from notes, scales and arpeggios into chords and progressions. Explore Dorian funk, Phrygian metal, Mixolydian ska, extended chords, modal interchange and smooth voice leading.

## Hooks and earworms
Use short recognizable motifs, rhythmic title repetition, call and response, instrumental counter-hooks, strategic silence, contrast, melodic leaps and transformed motifs. Balance recognition with surprise. Prosody aligns musical delivery with lyrical meaning.

## Council orchestration
Assign instruments roles rather than merely listing them. Rotate leadership, stagger entrances, separate registers, use selective doubling, counterpoint, hocketing, ostinatos, pedal tones and interlocking grooves. Distribute chord tones across instruments (e.g. Dm9: D F A C E). Silence is an assignment too.

## Rhythm and groove
Explore syncopation, ghost notes, hemiola, polymeter, metric displacement, three-over-four phrasing, clave, one-drop and offbeat accents. Preserve the central groove.

## Foniks Ling and IPA
Experimental: vowel elongation, rolled consonants, glottal stops, phonetic repetition, invented phonetic language and syllabic manipulation can influence vocal character. User-reported observation: an earlier IPA experiment produced a Chekov-like accent; exact input not recovered and repeatability unverified. Test phonetic vocal leitmotifs.

## Prompt engineering
Plan tonal center, mode, tempo, meter, progression, instrumental roles, dynamics and production. Separate Lyrics, Style and Exclusions. Change one variable at a time. Exact pitch, meter, tuning and instrumentation are not guaranteed by generative prompts.

## Mixing and mastering
Protect low-end clarity with kick/bass separation, centered sub-bass, audible harmonics, subtractive EQ, transient preservation, filtered reverb, mono checks and level-matched references. Avoid unnecessary processing. Bass led, brass fed, deep bottom end.

## Experiment log
Record date, model/version, prompt, settings, changed variable, output reference, observations and repeatability. Classify each entry as established musical theory, experimental prompting, user-reported observation, repeatably demonstrated technique or failed experiment. Never confuse theory with verified model behavior.
